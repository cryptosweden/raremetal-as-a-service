The Gap

Reserves you're asked to believe, not verify

Almost every "backed by physical metal" claim rests on a self-reported inventory list and an audit that was already stale the day it was filed. That gap is where every bullion shortfall in the last fifty years has hidden.

Today

The handshake model

  • Annual or quarterly audits, already outdated by publication
  • Static PDF certificates with no way to independently verify
  • Self-reported inventory, taken on faith
  • No way to detect a bar pledged twice, in two places
With Cupel

  • Attestations on a cadence the custodian sets — weekly or monthly
  • Cryptographic hash anchored publicly, evidence stays private
  • Automated + human-reviewed verification on every submission
  • Network-wide double-pledge detection on every holding ID
Architecture

The Metal Verification Network

Custodians, assayers, and auditors submit signed attestations. What gets published is a hash and a signature — enough to verify a claim is real, not enough to expose the vault behind it.

01

Custodian submits evidence

Photo or video, weight, purity, and serial data — captured at the time of attestation, signed by the submitting party.

02

Automated + human review

Media authenticity, consistency with prior submissions, and cross-network duplication checks run first. Anything inconclusive routes to a human assayer before acceptance.

03

Hash anchored, evidence stays private

The holding ID, evidence hash, metadata hash, signature, and timestamp are published. The photo itself never leaves the custodian's hands.

04

Anyone can verify, no one can see the vault

A lender, buyer, or regulator checks that the claim holds — without receiving inventory lists, client identities, or vault locations.

The Output

Proof of Reserve

Three distinct claims, stated explicitly rather than implied — because "the gold exists" and "you own the gold" are not the same fact.

Claim A

Possession

This specific bar or lot exists and was verified genuine at the given timestamp. Says nothing about who owns it.

Existence only
Claim B

Ownership

This entity holds legal claim to the attested holding. Requires the custodian to attest allocation, not just custody.

Existence + allocation
Claim C

Aggregate Value

Total holdings are worth at least X, without revealing which bars, how many, or where. Built for solvency, not inventory disclosure.

Value without detail
The Product

RareMetal-as-a-Service

The framework that turns a holding into an ongoing proof feed — register it, attach a custodian, set a cadence, and share a record that updates itself.

Built for
  • Custodians offering verifiable holdings as a real differentiator
  • Funds and lenders using metal as collateral
  • Individual holders who want a checkable record without going public
Deliberately not
  • A custodian — RMaaS verifies attestations, it doesn't hold metal
  • A pricing or trading venue
  • A substitute for real-world chain of custody
Stated Plainly

Photo and video evidence is easier to forge than a wallet signature, and a single frame proves a bar existed in front of a camera — not that it's allocated to you, or that the same bar isn't being shown elsewhere. We treat this as a real limit of a v1 system. Serialized bar registries, tamper-evident tagging, and independent assayer sign-off are on the roadmap specifically because photo evidence alone isn't the end state.
