RareMetal-as-a-Service

Metal Verification Network (MVN) and a Proof-of-Reserve framework for auditable, privacy-preserving attestations of physical precious metals.

Overview

Today's gold vaults mostly rely on a "trust us" model. RareMetal-as-a-Service aims to change that by providing verifiable, auditable attestations that a metal holding actually exists without exposing private media or full inventories.

Core pieces

  • MVN (Metal Verification Network) — attestors (vault custodians, assayers, auditors) submit signed proofs of metal holdings (weight, purity, serial/bar numbers) without exposing full inventories.
  • RareMetal-as-a-Service — framework for registering a metal holding, linking it to a custodian attestation, and issuing a verifiable "proof of reserve" that others can check on-chain without seeing private media.
  • Marketplace layer (optional) — surface metal-backed lending, redemption, or trading opportunities once base verification exists.

Problems this project addresses

  • Custodian trust model: metals aren't natively on-chain like a wallet balance — custodians or federated attestors must prove stored metal is real and allocated.
  • Proof granularity: different proofs for possession (bar exists and is authentic), ownership (claim to it), and value (aggregate worth without revealing specific bars) are required.

Approach (AI-assisted verification)

  1. Custodian uploads photo/video of metal holding (weekly/monthly, their choice).
  2. AI vision model evaluates: does this look like real bars/coins, do visible serials/hallmarks match registered values, and is the submission consistent with previous submissions.
  3. AI verdict + hash of the media + metadata → signed attestation by custodian.
  4. Attestation (hash only, not the raw media) → written on-chain as a Proof-of-Reserve record.
  5. Anyone can verify: on-chain hash exists with timestamp, custodian signature, and AI confidence score — without seeing the original photo or full inventory.
